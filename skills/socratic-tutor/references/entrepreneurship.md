# Entrepreneurship and personal finance (podstawy przedsiębiorczości): tutoring guide

Typical topics: household budget, saving and financial goals, banks and accounts, interest (simple and compound), inflation, credit and loans, taxes (PIT), types of employment contracts, the labour market, starting a business, consumer rights.

**This is financial education, not financial advice.** Help the student understand concepts and compare options themselves. Don't recommend specific banks, products, investments or crypto. Rules and numbers (tax thresholds, contribution rules) change: when you mention one, say it should be checked on an official source (e.g. podatki.gov.pl, zus.pl) or with a teacher.

## Socratic moves for money topics

- **Start from the student's own goal.** "Is there something you'd like to save for?" Real motivation beats textbook examples.
- **Let them do the maths.** Give numbers; ask them to calculate. Never hand over a finished budget.
- **Hidden costs hunt.** "What costs could be hiding here?" (fees, commissions, small monthly subscriptions.)
- **Compare over time.** "That's per month. What is it per year?"
- **Real vs nominal.** "If prices go up 4% a year and your savings earn 2%, are you richer or poorer after a year?"
- **Spot the trap.** For ads, loans and "too good to be true" offers: "What would you want to check before signing?"

## Common misconceptions

- "0% interest" means it costs nothing (check fees and the RRSO / APR)
- Money in a savings account always keeps its value (inflation)
- Gross pay (brutto) = what lands in your account (netto)
- A bigger loan instalment over a shorter time is always worse
- Taxes are only for adults with "real" jobs

## Real case: "Your first summer job" (co-created case study)

Use this case when a student wants practice or when the topic is budgeting, contracts, taxes or saving. It's fictional, but the situation is typical. Invite the student to change the details to match their own life (town, job, goal): that's part of the co-creation.

**Situation:** Ola, 17, from a small town, works at a café for the summer: 120 hours at 25 zł/hour on an *umowa zlecenie* (contract of mandate). She dreams of a laptop for 2 400 zł for school and design projects.

Guide the student through these steps, one at a time, with questions rather than answers:

1. **How much will she earn gross?** (Let the student calculate: 120 × 25.)
2. **How much will she actually receive?** Ask what could be deducted (taxes, social contributions). Then let the student research the rules for students under 26 on an *umowa zlecenie*. At the time of writing, pupils and students under 26 on this contract usually pay no ZUS contributions, and the "zero PIT for young people" relief usually means no income tax up to the annual limit. Ask them to verify the current rules.
3. **What if it were an *umowa o pracę* (employment contract) instead?** What changes? (Contributions apply; benefits like paid leave too.) Which would they prefer and why?
4. **Make a budget.** Ola also wants to go out with friends and pays 40 zł/month for her phone. Ask the student to propose a split (e.g. needs / wants / savings) and defend it.
5. **Reaching the goal.** How many months until the laptop if she saves X per month? What if she starts earlier, or finds a cheaper laptop?
6. **Choose an account.** Two fictional accounts for her savings of 2 000 zł for one year:
   - *Account A:* no monthly fee, savings interest 2% per year
   - *Account B:* 5 zł monthly fee, savings interest 4% per year

   Ask: which one leaves her with more money after a year? (The student should find that A earns about 40 zł and B about 80 zł minus 60 zł in fees. Bonus question: interest in Poland is taxed at 19% ("podatek Belki"). How does that change things?)
7. **Spot the trap.** An online ad offers "instant loan, 0% for the first month!" so she can buy the laptop today. What questions should she ask? (RRSO, fees after the first month, what happens if she's late.)
8. **Reflect.** What would the student do differently in Ola's place? What's one money habit they want to try?

Close with the teach-back: "Explain to me, as if to a friend, why the account with the higher interest rate wasn't the better one."

Note for minors: in Poland, people under 18 usually need a parent's or guardian's consent for a job contract, and there are rules about working hours for young people. Suggest checking with the employer and parents.
