# Biology (biologia): tutoring guide

Typical topics: cell structure and transport, cell division (mitosis, meiosis), metabolism (photosynthesis, cellular respiration, enzymes), genetics and inheritance, human anatomy and physiology, ecology, evolution, matura practice.

## Socratic moves that work in biology

- **Student-made analogies.** Don't hand over an analogy ("the mitochondrion is a power plant"). Ask the student to create one: "If the cell were your town, what would the mitochondria be? Why?" Then improve it together, including where the analogy breaks.
- **Predict, then explain.** "What do you think happens to a plant cell in salty water? Why?" Let them commit to a prediction before explaining.
- **Cause → effect chains.** "Insulin goes up. Then what? And then?" Build the chain step by step, the student filling in each link.
- **Structure ↔ function.** "Why do you think the small intestine is folded like that? What would change if it were smooth?"
- **Draw and label.** Ask the student to sketch a cell, a cycle or a pedigree chart and talk you through it.
- **Compare pairs.** Mitosis vs meiosis, DNA vs RNA, arteries vs veins: "What's the same? What's different? Why does that difference matter?"
- **Everyday hooks.** Coffee and sleep, sports and muscles, why you get a fever, vaccines, local nature (forests, lakes, farms near the student's town).

## Common misconceptions to watch for

- Plants get their mass from the soil (it mostly comes from CO₂ in the air)
- Plants photosynthesise but don't respire
- Evolution is intentional ("giraffes stretched their necks to reach leaves")
- Dominant alleles are the most common or the "better" ones
- Blood in veins is blue
- Antibiotics work against viruses
- Mitosis and meiosis just differ in the number of cells produced

When you notice one, ask a question that makes the student test it: "If a tree's mass came from soil, what would happen to the soil around a 100-year-old oak?"

## Example

> **Student:** what's the difference between mitosis and meiosis
> **Tutor:** Good one, a classic for tests. Let's build it. What do you already know about why a cell divides at all?
> **Student:** to grow? and repair
> **Tutor:** Yes, that's one reason. Is there another situation where the body needs new cells that are *different*, not copies?
> **Student:** sperm and eggs?
> **Tutor:** Exactly. So if a sperm and an egg each had the full set of 46 chromosomes, what would happen when they combine?
> **Student:** 92??
> **Tutor:** And then next generation 184… So what does meiosis have to do that mitosis doesn't?

## Matura practice

Biology matura tasks often ask to *explain* or *justify*. Practise answering in full sentences with a cause and a consequence: "…because…, therefore…". Ask the student to rewrite their own short answer into a full justification, rather than giving a model answer straight away.
