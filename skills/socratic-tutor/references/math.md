# Maths (matematyka): tutoring guide

Typical liceum/technikum topics: linear and quadratic functions, equations and inequalities, powers and roots, logarithms, sequences, trigonometry, planimetry and stereometry, probability and statistics, matura practice (podstawowa / rozszerzona).

## Socratic moves that work in maths

- **Estimate first.** "Before calculating: should the answer be bigger or smaller than 10? Positive or negative?"
- **Draw it.** Ask the student to sketch the graph, triangle or situation. "What would this look like on a picture?"
- **Try real numbers.** When algebra feels abstract: "Let's put x = 2 and see what happens."
- **Name the goal.** "What are we looking for? What would we need to know to get it?"
- **Find your own mistake.** When the answer is wrong, don't correct it. "Your answer is off. Check each line. Where could it go wrong?" If needed, narrow it down: "The first three lines are fine."
- **Check the answer.** "How can we check this without redoing it? Plug it back in?"
- **Connect to life.** Percentages → sales and discounts; functions → phone plans; probability → games; sequences → savings. Ask the student for their own example first.

## Common misconceptions to watch for

- (a + b)² = a² + b²
- Multiplying an inequality by a negative number without flipping the sign
- √(a + b) = √a + √b
- "Percent of a percent": a 20% rise followed by a 20% drop gives the original price
- Confusing the domain of a function with its set of values (dziedzina vs zbiór wartości)
- Probability: "two outcomes means 50/50"
- Losing solutions when dividing both sides by an expression that can be zero

When you spot one, don't name it as an error straight away. Ask a question that makes the student test it: "Let's check with a = 1, b = 2. Does (1+2)² equal 1² + 2²?"

## Example (hint ladder in action)

> **Student:** solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0, I don't get it
> **Tutor:** Let's crack it together. What kind of equation is this? What's the highest power of x?
> **Student:** x squared so quadratic
> **Tutor:** Yes. What tools do you know for quadratic equations? Any you've seen in class?
> **Student:** delta?
> **Tutor:** Delta works. There's also a shortcut: can you find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add up to 5?
> **Student:** 2 and 3
> **Tutor:** Nice. So how could you rewrite x² − 5x + 6 using 2 and 3?
> …
> **Tutor:** Now check: put x = 2 into the original equation. Does it give 0?

## Matura practice

Ask whether the student is preparing for the basic (podstawowa) or extended (rozszerzona) level. Encourage them to time themselves on full tasks, and to explain their reasoning in words, because examiners award points for the method. Don't promise how tasks will be graded: tell them to check the official CKE materials or ask their teacher.
